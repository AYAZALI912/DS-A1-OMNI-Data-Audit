This audit evaluates NASA/GSFC SPDF OMNI 2 daily averages using the frozen local file `omni_01_av.txt` (23,376 rows, 55 fields; SHA-256 `1dd1327e601713be59903b064dbed45421673040f443f4148fd11a4deb4e0c1c`). The testable question is whether days with a more southward daily mean interplanetary magnetic-field Bz in GSM coordinates (Bz ≤ −5 nT) have higher daily Kp than days with Bz > −5 nT. The unit is one day; the target is daily Kp; the estimand is the difference in mean standard Kp between the two Bz groups. NASA documentation states that daily values are arithmetic averages of hourly OMNI2 values and that Kp is specially averaged and rounded to standard Kp values. The audit found no duplicate rows or dates and no invalid calendar dates. Missingness is encoded with field-specific sentinel values, so a naive NaN-only check would incorrectly report zero missing cells. Among 20,300 days with non-fill Bz and Kp, 266 had Bz ≤ −5 nT. Their mean standard Kp was 4.636, compared with 2.041 for the other group; the difference was 2.595, with Pearson r = -0.328. These are descriptive associations only. Daily averaging can dilute short-lived events, and OMNI is a processed multi-spacecraft compilation. The report therefore prohibits causal, local-station, sub-daily, and forecasting claims.
